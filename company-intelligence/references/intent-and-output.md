# Intent and output

## Competitor intelligence

Answer: what did the competitor change, and can it threaten or enable us?

Organize as `competitor -> verified change -> capability/strategy delta -> customer impact -> threat/opportunity -> response`. Include a concise battlecard: where they are stronger, where they remain vulnerable, likely sales claims, counter-positioning grounded in evidence, and watch items. Never invent competitive claims.

## Account intelligence

Answer: what changed at this customer, why now, and how should we engage?

Organize as `customer -> business change -> strategic priority -> pain/trigger -> IT implication -> opportunity hypothesis -> next engagement`. For each opportunity give evidence, likely sponsor/user/blocker, discovery question, recommended action, timing signal, confidence, and disqualifier. Do not claim budget or vendor intent without evidence.

## Market intelligence

Answer: which changes repeat across the market and what do they imply?

Separate company-specific events from cross-company patterns. State sample, period, geography, leading/lagging indicators, counterexamples, market implication, whitespace, and watch list.

## Partner intelligence

Answer: where can the organizations create differentiated joint value, and what could block it?

Organize as `strategic fit -> complementary assets -> overlap/conflict -> joint use cases -> required commitments -> stakeholders -> next engagement`. Separate a public partnership announcement from verified joint execution or customer adoption.

## Investment intelligence

Answer: how is the enterprise performing against its strategy, and which catalysts, dependencies, and risks matter?

Organize as `thesis context -> performance signals -> strategy execution -> catalysts -> risks/counterevidence -> monitoring questions`. Preserve accounting definitions and reporting periods. Distinguish management guidance from measured results and do not provide personalized financial advice.

## General brief

Give a balanced view: baseline, key changes, financial/operating signals, strategy, technology, competitors, implications, opportunities/risks, next actions.

## Audience formats

- Executive: 5-7 takeaways, significance, scenarios, decisions, and watch items.
- Account insight: triggers, stakeholder map, opportunity hypotheses, discovery questions, and next engagement.
- Sales: battlecard, value hypotheses, objections, proof needed, and actions.
- Strategy: strategic choices, capability gaps, scenarios, dependencies, and decisions.
- Investment: execution signals, catalysts, risks, counterevidence, and watch items.
- Technical: current-state evidence, likely requirements, architecture/integration/security implications, constraints, and validation questions.

## Default structure

1. Scope, entity, period, intent, and as-of date.
2. Executive summary.
3. Prioritized changes: date, verified fact, status, enterprise dimension, importance, confidence, source.
4. What changed and why it matters.
5. Intent-specific analysis.
6. Opportunities, threats, or risks mapped to verified triggers.
7. Next actions with owners/stakeholders and questions.
8. Uncertainties, contradictions, and evidence gaps.
9. Direct source links.
