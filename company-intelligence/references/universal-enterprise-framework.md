# Universal enterprise framework

Use this framework before any industry pack. Select relevant dimensions; do not fill every category mechanically.

## Enterprise baseline

- Identity: legal entity, brands, ownership, headquarters, operating geographies, reporting perimeter.
- Business model: customers, value proposition, products/services, revenue model, channels, partners, and critical resources.
- Portfolio: business units, segments, major products, growth engines, declining or non-core activities.
- Position: market role, differentiators, customer concentration, competitors, substitutes, and ecosystem power.
- Leadership and governance: decision makers, ownership, board, leadership changes, incentives, and declared priorities.
- Performance: relevant financial, customer, operational, workforce, and risk indicators with definitions and reporting periods.

## Universal change taxonomy

Classify each event under one primary dimension and optional secondary dimensions:

1. Strategy and capital allocation: priorities, portfolio choices, investment, divestment, restructuring, M&A.
2. Financial performance: revenue, profit, cash flow, margin, guidance, funding, cost, and capital structure.
3. Customers and market: demand, customer growth/loss, pricing, channels, experience, brand, and market share.
4. Products and innovation: launches, roadmap, R&D, intellectual property, packaging, and commercialization.
5. Operations and supply chain: capacity, sourcing, logistics, quality, productivity, resilience, and footprint.
6. Geography and expansion: market entry/exit, facilities, localization, regulation, and regional partnerships.
7. Organization and talent: leadership, workforce, operating model, skills, culture, and incentives.
8. Ecosystem and transactions: suppliers, alliances, distribution, platforms, acquisitions, and joint ventures.
9. Risk and external environment: legal, regulatory, geopolitical, financial, reputational, cyber, and sustainability risks.
10. Technology and digital capability: applications, platforms, data, AI, cloud, automation, infrastructure, security, and governance.

## Universal analysis questions

For each material change ask:

- What changed relative to the prior baseline, commitment, or market expectation?
- Is this a statement, allocation of resources, implementation milestone, adoption signal, or measured outcome?
- Which growth, cost, risk, customer, capability, or operating-model objective does it support?
- Which business unit, geography, customer group, process, or stakeholder is affected?
- What prerequisites, dependencies, constraints, and competing explanations exist?
- Is the signal isolated, repeated, accelerating, delayed, or contradicted?
- What decision does this enable for the user's stated intent?

## Optional technology lens

Apply only when requested or supported by the evidence. Translate `business trigger -> required capability -> current-state evidence -> gap/hypothesis -> stakeholder -> validation question -> possible response`. Keep vendor/product mapping last. A business change may have no meaningful technology implication; say so when appropriate.
